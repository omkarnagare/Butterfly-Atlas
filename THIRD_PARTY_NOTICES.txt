1. DATA
-------

1.1 Taxonomy (the butterfly list of India)
    Names, common names, ranks and the family tree come from iNaturalist (https://www.inaturalist.org): the species list
    is built from verifiable iNaturalist observations in India and its taxon data, by the developer's taxonomy-importer
    tool, and shipped as the plain, checksum-verified file taxonomy/taxonomy-india-YYYY-MM-DD.json inside the app. The
    data is not obfuscated, encrypted or hidden. No iNaturalist photographs are included.

    What the file holds: scientific names, common names, ranks, iNaturalist taxon numbers and synonyms, and the fact
    that a species has verifiable observations in India (the data was retrieved on 2026-10-08). It holds no photographs,
    no observation records, no observer names and no coordinates.

    iNaturalist's Terms of Use (https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/terms) say that content its users contribute is, unless
    the contributor chooses otherwise, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial (CC BY-NC); each
    observation, photograph and sound carries its own licence. None of that content is included here. iNaturalist's
    published terms do not state a separate licence for taxon names or taxonomy data, and common names were contributed by
    its users. The data was retrieved through iNaturalist's public API under its Terms of Service and is credited here.
    Butterfly Atlas is not endorsed by iNaturalist. This data is not covered by Butterfly Atlas's Apache License.

1.2 Place names and boundaries
    Place names and boundaries bundled with Butterfly Atlas (places-2026-10-16.json and .bin)
    
    Built by the developer tool place-importer from these sources:
    
    1. geoBoundaries gbOpen, India ADM2 (districts), release of 2023-12-12
       https://github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries/raw/9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d/releaseData/gbOpen/IND/ADM2/geoBoundaries-IND-ADM2.geojson
       Runfola, D. et al. (2020) geoBoundaries: A global database of political administrative
       boundaries. PLoS ONE 15(4): e0231866. https://www.geoboundaries.org
       Upstream: Pathways Data Pvt. Ltd. (geonode.pathwaysdata.com), lgdirectory.gov.in.
       Licence: Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0,
       https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/
       The boundaries and the place list in these files are a Derived Database of it and are made
       available under the ODbL 1.0.
    
    2. geoBoundaries gbOpen, India ADM1 (states and union territories), release of 2023-12-12
       https://github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries/raw/9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d/releaseData/gbOpen/IND/ADM1/geoBoundaries-IND-ADM1.geojson
       Upstream: DataMeet India community (https://github.com/datameet/maps), Election
       Commission of India. Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 2.5 India (CC BY 2.5 IN),
       https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/in/
    
    3. Natural Earth v5.1.2, 1:10m Admin 0 countries (India's point of view) and Admin 1 states and
       provinces. Made with Natural Earth. Free vector and raster map data @ naturalearthdata.com.
       Public domain. https://www.naturalearthdata.com
    
    4. Local Government Directory (LGD), Ministry of Panchayati Raj, Government of India
       (https://lgdirectory.gov.in): district names and LGD codes, from the list districts.12Oct2026.csv
       (SHA-256 ff37eb5ac4fdfad369ef792b13410fff635294f50104b1061fc94b5800be4b2b). Indian district names follow the LGD spelling where a
       boundary matches an LGD district; the boundary's own spelling is kept as an alias.
    
    Changes: borders noded into one coverage on a 1e-5 degree grid and simplified (about 100 m in
    India, about 1 km elsewhere); disputed areas follow India's official view (Natural Earth's
    India point of view); Indian states and districts clipped to India's outline; areas of India
    that geoBoundaries gives no district are areas of their state.

5. HEIC PHOTOS: THE COMPUTER'S OWN DECODER (not included)
---------------------------------------------------------

  Butterfly Atlas includes no HEIC, HEIF or HEVC picture decoder. To show a HEIC photo it starts, as a separate
  program, a decoder that the computer's vendor or the user provides: sips on macOS, Windows' own HEIF and HEVC
  decoders on Windows (through Windows PowerShell), and heif-convert on Linux. Using that decoder is covered by the
  licence of whoever provides it (the computer's vendor, or the user who installed it), not by Butterfly Atlas, which
  grants no rights in it. Where a decoder is restricted by patents in a country, obtaining and using it lawfully is a
  matter for the user and the decoder's supplier. Without a decoder, HEIC photos are still kept exactly as they are.

  The app carries two tiny HEIC test pictures (about 800 bytes each, simple shapes drawn by Butterfly Atlas's own
  code) that it asks the decoder to show, only to check that the decoder works and whether it turns pictures itself.
  Photo details such as the date and GPS position are read from HEIC files by Butterfly Atlas's own code and
  metadata-extractor (listed above); no picture is decoded for that.
